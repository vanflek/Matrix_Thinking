ONE RULE SET, EVERYWHERE

Describe the materials.
Let the world happen.

  1. Sand tries to fall with gravity, then diagonally. It can swap with water, so grains sink.
  2. Water tries gravity, then diagonals, then the two perpendicular directions. It never moves against gravity on its own.
  3. Walls stay put. The exterior is blocked; nothing wraps or disappears.
  4. Conflicts are resolved with whole-field masks. Gravity has priority over diagonals, then sideways spreading. Within a directional pass, swaps claim both cells before ordinary moves. Competing proposals wait.

Diagonal moves cannot squeeze between two walls that touch at a corner. Turn diagonals off to see why sand then forms columns. Turn spreading off to see what water can no longer do.

There is no instruction saying “make a pile” or “fill this bowl.” Shapes emerge from repeated transformations of W.

Directional matrix passes in this step

At most nine passes act on the entire field. Two alternating checkerboard masks choose which side to try first. The physics loop visits directions, not cells.

    SHIFT · COMPARE · MASK · UPDATE

    The whole field.
    In every expression.

    # One directional pass of the full NumPy model.
# dr, dc: direction; scope: checkerboard/all-field mask.
# All masks and W have the same 2-D shape.
empty_ahead = shift(W == 0, -dr, -dc)
can_swap = (W == 1) & shift(W == 2, -dr, -dc)
proposed = remaining & scope & allowed & (
    empty_ahead | can_swap)
remaining &= ~proposed

free = ~claimed
swaps = proposed & can_swap & free & shift(free, -dr, -dc)
claimed |= swaps | shift(swaps, dr, dc)

free = ~claimed
ordinary = proposed & empty_ahead & free & shift(free, -dr, -dc)
accepted = swaps | ordinary
destinations = shift(accepted, dr, dc)
claimed |= ordinary | shift(ordinary, dr, dc)

old_values = shift(W, dr, dc)
next_W[ordinary] = 0
next_W[destinations] = old_values[destinations]
next_W[swaps] = 2

    A shift aligns neighbours with their sources. Comparisons build eligibility masks; Boolean operations resolve competing claims. A translation has unique destinations within one direction. Claimed-cell masks prevent overlaps between directional passes.
