ONE RULE SET, EVERYWHERE

Describe the materials.
Let the world happen.

  1. Sand tries to fall with gravity, then diagonally. It can swap with water, so grains sink.
  2. Water tries gravity, then diagonals, then the two perpendicular directions. It never moves against gravity on its own.
  3. Walls stay put. The exterior is blocked; nothing wraps or disappears.
  4. Conflicts are resolved: each accepted move claims both cells. A competing proposal waits until a later step.

Diagonal moves cannot squeeze between two walls that touch at a corner. Turn diagonals off to see why sand then forms columns. Turn spreading off to see what water can no longer do.

There is no instruction saying “make a pile” or “fill this bowl.” Shapes emerge from repeated transformations of W.

THINK IN ARRAYS

A condition becomes a mask.

# Example: DOWN gravity, straight falls only.
# W contains IDs: 0 empty, 1 sand, 2 water, 3 wall.
below_empty = np.zeros(W.shape, dtype=bool)
below_empty[:-1, :] = (W[1:, :] == 0)

mobile = (W == 1) | (W == 2)
fall = mobile & below_empty
source_r, source_c = np.nonzero(fall)

next_W = W.copy()
next_W[source_r, source_c] = 0
next_W[source_r + 1, source_c] = W[fall]
W = next_W

The comparison selects empty destinations for the entire field. The mask selects all particles that can fall. For straight falls, destinations are unique. Adding diagonals, spreading and swaps requires the conflict-resolution stage shown above.

Interactive JavaScript cellular model; Python is not executed here. The NumPy excerpt is the straight-fall subrule, not the complete simulator. This is a discrete teaching model, not a pressure-based fluid solver. Water can remain above the bottom in a sealed bowl; it need not form a perfectly flat surface. Painting adds or removes particles; simulation steps conserve them.
